Four ways to make it compact and intentional

Measured on the Kreeper league this week before mocking: every tab's height, sections, tables, captions and card styles. The mocks use real data where it exists: your waiver run, your RB call, your playoff odds, this week's free agents, the Ballers' ranks and Sleeper's 24h adds. Projections, the +You figures and the bid ranges in the free-agent table are illustrative. Each is shown at desktop width since that's where the clutter lives; the phone shell already hides the header chrome.

1One bar, one strip

Before any content, every tab stacks three rows of chrome and a row of four tiles: the logo and league chips, the week and league facts, eight tab pills and five Sleeper links, then FAAB, weeks left, upgrades and roster size. Two of those tiles don't change a decision.

This collapses it to one bar, one tab row and a single strip of the facts that tab needs. The five Sleeper links become one, and it opens the page for the tab you're on.

2Lead with the answer, fold the rest

Waivers is 2,829px with five sections and four tables. Lineup is 1,906px with four sections. Each tab answers one question, but it hands you the evidence before the answer.

Every tab instead opens on its answer as a sentence (Waivers: the claim to make, or that there isn't one; Lineup: the moves; Trades: the best deal) with the supporting sections folded into drawers that summarise themselves.

3One player list per tab

The same free agents show up in up to four tables on one tab: the Ballers' list, ours, Trending, and the drop list. Rankings and Lineup have the same problem with each source getting its own block.

So: one table per tab, sources as columns, filters as pills. You pick the lens you want on the same rows instead of scrolling past three lists.

4One card grammar, help behind ⓘ

Each feature we built brought its own card: the recap, start/sit, playoffs, trade analyzer, inbox, keepers and claim builder each use a different header, number size and footer. And there are 16 explanatory captions across the tabs, most of them read once.

This gives everything one card grammar (kicker, headline sentence, three lines max, footnote) and moves the explanations behind ⓘ.
