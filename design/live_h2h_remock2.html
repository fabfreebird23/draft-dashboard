What changed, and why.
Pictures. Headshot from sleepercdn.com/content/nfl/players/thumb/{pid}.jpg with the team logo tucked into its inner corner — the logo carries the team, so the name line no longer has to shout it. Both are plain images on a CDN, no key, and the pid is the id we already join every league on. A missing headshot falls back to the tile you see behind it, which is what the CDN serves for a rookie anyway.
Numbers. One 72px tabular column per side, hard against the middle gutter: actual on top, projection underneath in the same column. Every row's numbers line up down the page whatever the name does, and the two sides face each other across the badge instead of drifting with the text. A man who has not played shows an em dash, not a 0.0 — 0.0 is a score, and he hasn't got one yet.
Stats. One mono line, always the same order — efficiency, yards, touchdowns, then the second phase (16/24 · 204 pass · 3 TD · 10 car, 66 yd · 2 rush TD) — clipped with an ellipsis rather than wrapped, so a busy game cannot make the row taller than a quiet one.
The game. Always two pieces: a clock pill (Q4 10:53 live, SUN 4:25 before, FINAL after) and the score, with a dot on the team that has the ball. Weather, a dome, an injury and "locks last" are the same small outline chip, so the eye learns one shape.
