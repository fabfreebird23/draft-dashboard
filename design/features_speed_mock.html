The features use real Kreeper data: your roster, the 3-1 start, tprib as this week's opponent, and the FAAB history. The odds and points on the feature screens are illustrative. The speed numbers were measured today on this machine with the app's own timing harness: per-tab page size, and a cold Live open counted call by call. The one exception is S3: the phone tab-switch timeline is an estimate, because the phone can't be timed from here.

01Playoff odds and the must-win week

You can see this week's win %, but not where the season is heading. This runs the rest of the schedule 10,000 times from the projections the app already has, and shows your playoff odds, where you're likely to finish, and the week that matters most.

The must-win card is the useful part. It names the one game that moves your odds most, so you know which week to spend FAAB for or to make a trade before.

Monte Carlo over the remaining matchups using Live's team_distribution (mean + SD per lineup); reads playoff_teams from Sleeper settings; ESPN TD's via its schedule API. Under a second for 10k seasons in numpy, cached per week. About a day.

02Trade desk: this year and next

All three keeper leagues trade, and a trade changes two things: this year's lineup and next year's keeper price. The app already works out both, but in separate places. This puts them on one screen.

Add players to each side and it re-runs both lineups (you and them), your playoff odds, and the keeper price each player carries into 2027. It also tells you whether they'd likely accept, based on what the trade does to their lineup.

Reuses LU.optimize for both rosters, the playoff sim from 01 (so do 01 first or alongside), and keeper_outlook's ladder for the incoming players. Acceptance = how much their lineup changes. A new More-sheet screen. 1–1.5 days.

03Waiver recap: did the bid work?

The new FAAB bids are only good if they're checked. When waivers run, this shows what you won and lost, what the runner-up bid, and what the app suggested, so you can see whether to trust the next suggestion.

Over the season it adds up wins, total spend and how much you overpaid the runner-up. Each week's claims feed back into the model, so it keeps up when the league starts bidding more.

Sleeper transactions for the week just run (already fetched by faabmarket) + the suggestion saved when the claim was queued (userstate). Feeds faabmarket's current-season rows. Half a day.

04Start/sit, side by side

Lineup says who to start but not why it's close. Tap two players and this lines them up: floor, median and ceiling, matchup, role, kickoff time, and what each choice does to your win probability.

The win-% edge is the number that matters. Two backs 0.9 projected points apart can be 3 points of win % apart once your opponent's lineup is counted, or zero.

player_sd + win_prob already exist (swing_players uses them); DvP from positional.py; snap/carry share from nflstats. Tap-two-to-compare on Lineup, rendered as a sheet. About a day.

S1Send the stylesheet once, not on every page

Each page carries the app's whole stylesheet, 157KB, as text in the page. Streamlit sends it again on every tab, every rerun and every two-minute refresh. Measured: every tab is 182–206KB, and about 157KB of that is the same CSS.

Serving it once as a file means the browser and the phone's WebView download it once and cache it. Every page after that is about 30KB.

theme.CSS → static/theme..css, served with Streamlit's static file serving and linked once; the hash changes on a deploy so nobody gets stale styles. Small. Risk: Cloud's /~/+/ iframe path, so check it on Cloud first.

S2Make Live's first open fetch in parallel

The first time Live opens after the server starts, it waits 27.5s on 50 web calls made one at a time: 7 ESPN calls (19s, the TD's league), 16 Sleeper projection reads (12s), and some small ones.

Run them together and the wait drops to the slowest single call, about 3s. Add them to the warmer on game days and even the first open is a cache hit (0.3s, as measured).

A ThreadPoolExecutor around the per-week/per-team loops in the ESPN rosters and Sleeper projection fetchers; warm.py adds Live's inputs on game days. Small to medium. Measured before/after with the same harness.

S3Switch phone tabs without reloading

On the phone, every tab tap loads a new URL, which means a new Streamlit session, a new websocket, the whole script, and the whole stylesheet again. The freeze-frame hides it, but it still takes about 3 seconds.

Instead, the Android shell (and the Mac app) keep one session open and press the hidden tab button through a JS bridge, so only that tab redraws. Deep links still work for alerts and cold starts.

MainActivity: a tab tap runs JS that clicks the hidden .st-key-navbar button for that tab; falls back to the URL if the bridge isn't ready. app.py keeps deep links for alerts. Medium. Biggest change to how the phone feels.

S4Live scores without redrawing the page

On Sundays Live refreshes the whole page every 2 minutes to change about a dozen numbers, and the scores can be 2 minutes old.

A small fragment holding just the score strip and the slot points refreshes every 20 seconds and sends about 3KB, not 186KB. Changed numbers flash once. The rest of the board stays put, so nothing jumps while you're reading it.

@st.fragment(run_every=20) around the h2h score strip only, fed by the ESPN scoreboard + Sleeper live stats already cached for 20s; the rest of Live drops to the slow clock. Small to medium. Watch Sleeper's rate limit on 4 leagues.
