Where else the card fits

The test each screen had to pass: two sides, a number that settles it, a reason. Where that's true the port is nearly free — a matchup is a game, a trade is two sides, a waiver is a swap. Where it isn't, the screen is left alone; the board grid, the rankings list and the queue are all better as dense rows.

Left aloneon purpose

The draft board grid, the rankings list, the queue and the pick predictor all fail the test — one side, no verdict, and their whole job is letting you scan forty rows at once. A card costs six times the height to say the same thing. They keep their rows.
